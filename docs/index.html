Vocoder! Strings! Voices! Organ!

Free-solo ensemble. Forty-four keys. No rope.

A sound generator for Schwung on Ableton Move. Ten bands of vocoder, paraphonic strings, a human choir and a square-wave organ, all through a bucket-brigade style ensemble, then a three-band equalizer and a small stereo reverb. It follows the Roland VP-330 and gains the organ and string registers of the RS-09.

What it isA choir and an orchestra on one keyboard

VDP33 plays three sections from the same divide-down oscillators: Strings, Human Voice (four formant choirs) and, new in 0.3, an Organ of four square-wave feet. A fourth path, the Vocoder, imposes the spectrum of whatever enters the Move's audio input onto the keys. Everything is written from scratch in one C file; there is no code, sample or ROM from any original instrument.

The keys are paraphonic: every note of a pitch class is made from one master oscillator through octave dividers, so chords stay phase-locked as on the originals. Waveforms are band-limited (PolyBLEP), and the whole thing costs about 7% of one desktop core in the worst case, 24 keys with everything on.

InstallBuild it, then put it on the Move

You need a Move with Schwung installed, and Docker or an aarch64-linux-gnu- toolchain to build.

bash scripts/build.sh      # makes dist/vdp33/ and dist/vdp33-module.tar.gz

Then either open Schwung Manager at http://move.local:7700, go to Modules and install a custom module from the tarball, or run bash scripts/install.sh to copy the folder over SSH. Restart the Move afterwards, because Schwung can keep an older copy of a module in memory.

Releases are built by GitHub Actions on every v* tag. The tarball in the release is what the Schwung module catalogue installs.

PlayFirst sound in a minute

  1. Hold Track 1-4 for half a second to open a slot, and choose VDP33 as the sound generator.
  2. On that Move track set MIDI Out to a channel from 1 to 4, check the slot's Receive Ch matches, and load an empty Drum Rack so the native sound stays quiet.
  3. Play the pads. The first preset, Strings, needs no microphone.
  4. For the vocoder pick the Vocoder preset, plug in headphones or a line-in cable, and speak.
The vocoder listens to the Move's audio input. With the built-in speaker on and nothing plugged in, Schwung's feedback guard bypasses the slot and a B appears. Plug in headphones or a cable and it comes back by itself. The Move has one input, so there is no separate external synth input: Ext In is the same signal as the vocoder modulator and shares Mic Level.

The screen has ten pages of up to eight knobs: Main, Balance, Keyboard, Organ, Strings, Envelope, Ensemble, Vibrato, Pitch and EQ and Reverb. Turn a knob and its value is shown in real units (milliseconds, cents, semitones).

The web editor

With VDP33 in a slot, open Schwung Manager at http://move.local:7700 and choose Remote UI. The instrument above appears there, live: drag a slider and the Move follows, turn a knob on the Move and the slider follows. Two tablets, Lower and Upper, send a section to either half of the keyboard, and the yellow line over the keys is the split point. Touch any control and the amber display shows its value in real units.

The same page runs as a demo without a Move: open the demo editor. It has a rough on-screen keyboard that plays a simplified browser version of Organ and Strings; Human Voice and the Vocoder are silent there, and the real sound is on the Move. Drag a slider or knob, use the wheel for small steps, hold Shift for fine ones, double-click to reset. From the keyboard use the arrows, Page Up and Page Down, Home and End.

The panelWhere everything lives

The nameplate row holds the Preset display and a small route diagram of the signal path. Below it, top to bottom: Output, Balance, Vibrato, Vocoder; Human Voice, Release, Strings; Organ, Ensemble, Pitch Shift; Tuning, Keys and Pedals, Equalizer, Reverb. The small grades on the section tags (5.6, 5.10a and so on) are decoration.

Balance and output

Strings, Human Voice, Organ and Direct Mic are the four sliders. Levels follow a square law, so the lower half of a slider is a quiet region. Ext in to ens. sends the audio input through the ensemble untouched. Tone tilts the whole mix darker or brighter around 1.2 kHz, up to 6 dB either way. Master Volume is the master.

Vocoder

The Lower and Upper tablets choose which half of the keyboard feeds it (both lit means All, neither means Off); the halves are split at Split Point. Mic Level is input gain from -12 to +24 dB, Tone a brightness control for the carrier, Sibilance adds a noise path so consonants stay clear, and Ensemble sends the vocoder through the chorus.

Human voice

Four tablets pick the choirs: Male 8' Lower, Male 4' Lower, Male 8' Upper and Female 4' Upper. Attack sets how fast they swell and Ensemble sends them through the chorus.

Strings RS-09

Register is VP-330 (the original 16', 8' and 4' mix), 8' alone, 4' alone, or 8' + 4'. Tone darkens or opens the strings, Attack is their swell, and Legato decides whether every new key swells again (Each key) or only the first key of a phrase does (First key). The strings always run through the ensemble. Two tablets, Lower and Upper, assign them to the keyboard halves.

Organ RS-09

Four sliders mix the feet 8', 4', 2' and 1' of square waves. Tone is I Soft or II Bright, Ensemble sends the organ through the chorus, and Lower and Upper assign it to the keyboard, so strings can sit under the split and an organ above it.

Release RS-09

One Release slider serves all sections. Mode has three settings:

  • Free: each key owns one envelope, and a key you replay picks its own tail up again. This is how VDP33 behaved before 0.3.
  • I Wait: when no key is held, a new note waits until the old tail has faded below about -35 dB.
  • II Hold: a replayed key gets a new voice while the old tail finishes on its own.

The editor draws the envelope of two notes for the mode you have chosen, on a small green scope under the instrument. A second scope draws the equalizer curve and how long the reverb tail lasts.

Ensemble RS-09

Mode is Standard (the original), I Dynamic (deeper, faster and wetter) or II Natural (shallower and calmer). Depth and Speed are fine trims on top.

Vibrato

Rate from 2 to 9 Hz, Delay time up to two seconds before it fades in, Depth up to ±60 cents. Vibrato always reaches Human Voice and Vocoder. The tablets On strings and On organ let it reach those sections too.

Pitch shift and tuning VP-330

Mode is Off / Pedal, Auto or Manual. In Auto, each key press drops the pitch by Pitch Set and it glides back up over Time. Manual holds a static shift set by the Pitch Down slider. Off / Pedal does nothing until CC 66 is held, then it acts like Auto. The shift now reaches all three sections. Tune is ±50 cents, and Reference A switches 440 to 442 Hz. Bend and Octave complete the section.

Keys and pedals

Split Point divides Lower from Upper (hold Shift on the buttons to move by an octave). Velocity is Fixed or Dynamic. Pedal (CC 64) has four settings:

  • Off: the pedal does nothing.
  • Gate: the vocoder sounds only while the pedal is down (the old VDP33 behaviour).
  • Hold: a true Vocoder Hold. Pressing it freezes the ten band levels, so the vocal colour keeps sounding without any input.
  • Release: the RS-09 Release pedal. With the pedal up, release is zero; with it down, the Release slider applies.

Equalizer and reverb 0.4

Three sliders for tone (Low, Mid, High) and three for the room (Mix, Size, Damping). Details are in their own section.

Preset

The amber display shows the preset; the arrows step through all 26. Choosing a preset resets every parameter to its default, applies the preset's values, and keeps Octave, Bend, Master Volume, Tune, Velocity, Reference A, Tone, the Equalizer and the Reverb as you set them.

Updates in 0.3What came from the RS-09 and the VP-330 manual

From the Roland RS-09

  • An Organ section with 8', 4', 2' and 1' sliders, tone I and II, and its own ensemble switch.
  • Strings registers 8' and 4', separate from the VP-330 mix.
  • Ensemble modes I and II, release modes I and II, the release pedal and the legato attack choice.
  • Ext In through the ensemble, a master Tone, a 442 Hz reference, and vibrato switches for organ and strings.
  • Nine presets, numbers 15 to 23, named RS .... The first fifteen are unchanged.

Corrected from the VP-330 owner's manual

  • Pitch shift reaches strings, voice and organ, not only voice and vocoder.
  • Tune is ±50 cents. It had been ±1 semitone.
  • Vocoder Hold now freezes the band levels. The old Gate behaviour stays as Gate.

Old saved states load as before: parameters that did not exist are filled with neutral defaults, and Release mode defaults to Free, so a patch made in 0.2 sounds like it did.

New in 0.4An equalizer and a small reverb

Both sit at the very end of the signal path, after the Master Tone and just before the output limiter, so they colour everything: strings, voices, organ, vocoder and the external input. They are deliberately simple. Neither belongs to the Roland originals.

Equalizer

  • Low: a shelf at 120 Hz.
  • Mid: a broad bell at 1 kHz.
  • High: a shelf at 6 kHz.
  • Each slider cuts or boosts by up to 12 dB; the middle of the slider is flat. A band left flat is skipped entirely, so a flat equalizer changes nothing in the sound.

Reverb

  • Mix: the amount. At zero the reverb is off, and its tail is cleared.
  • Size: how long the tail lasts, from about 0.6 s to about 4 s for a 60 dB fall.
  • Damping: darkens the tail, as soft furnishings would.
  • Eight comb filters and four allpass filters per side, with the right side slightly longer than the left, so the room is wide.

The three tone sliders and the three room sliders are yours: changing preset does not reset them, like Master Tone and Volume. Two new presets use them: Hall Strings and Valley Choir, which set a reverb and a gentle equalizer when you load them. On the Move they are on their own screen page, EQ and Reverb, next to Master Tone and Volume. The editor draws the equalizer curve and the reverb decay in the card under the instrument.

HonestyWhat the manuals say, and what is a guess

Confirmed manuals

  • The section layout, four Human Voice switches, one shared Release.
  • Separate Attack for voice and strings; Tone on strings and vocoder.
  • Organ feet, strings registers, ensemble and release modes, release pedal and legato attack exist on the RS-09.
  • Pitch shift reaches all sections; Tune is ±50 cents; Vocoder Hold exists.

Estimates not measured

  • Centre frequencies and Q of the ten bands; choir formants; register mixes.
  • Ensemble delay times, rates and the two mode scalings.
  • Organ tone filters and foot weights.
  • The equalizer corner frequencies (120 Hz, 1 kHz, 6 kHz), its ±12 dB range, and the reverb's size, damping and mix scaling. The two originals have neither effect, so these are my own simple choices.
  • The meaning of Release Mode I and II. The RS-09 manual words it ambiguously. I implemented I as Wait and II as Hold, and kept Free as the default.

Not modelled: the Gate Out and Organ Raw Out jacks, the L/M/H output switch, a second input, SysEx, analogue drift and BBD companding noise. The instrument picture is an illustration inspired by the two Roland originals; it is not a copy of either panel and carries no Roland artwork. The constants most worth tuning against a real unit are k_band_hz and BAND_Q, the register mix in vdp_render, the formant tables and the ensemble constants; they are isolated and commented in src/dsp/vdp33.c.

The native test, bash scripts/test.sh, renders every preset to WAV and checks pitch, levels, vocoder tracking, Hold, zones, organ feet and tone, strings registers, release modes, legato attack, pedals, ensemble modes, master tone, equalizer, reverb, old-state compatibility and CPU load. node tests/web_test.mjs drives the editor in a browser.
