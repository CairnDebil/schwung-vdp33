VDP33

Vocoder · Strings · Voices · Organ

A sound generator for Schwung on Ableton Move. Ten bands of vocoder, paraphonic strings, a human choir and a square-wave organ, all through a bucket-brigade style ensemble. It follows the Roland VP-330 and gains the organ and string registers of the RS-09.

What it isA choir and an orchestra on one keyboard

VDP33 plays three sections from the same divide-down oscillators: Strings, Human Voice (four formant choirs) and, new in 0.3, an Organ of four square-wave feet. A fourth path, the Vocoder, imposes the spectrum of whatever enters the Move's audio input onto the keys. Everything is written from scratch in one C file; there is no code, sample or ROM from any original instrument.

The keys are paraphonic: every note of a pitch class is made from one master oscillator through octave dividers, so chords stay phase-locked as on the originals. Waveforms are band-limited (PolyBLEP), and the whole thing costs about 7% of one desktop core in the worst case, 24 keys with everything on.

InstallBuild it, then put it on the Move

You need a Move with Schwung installed, and Docker or an aarch64-linux-gnu- toolchain to build.

bash scripts/build.sh      # makes dist/vdp33/ and dist/vdp33-module.tar.gz

Then either open Schwung Manager at http://move.local:7700, go to Modules and install a custom module from the tarball, or run bash scripts/install.sh to copy the folder over SSH. Restart the Move afterwards, because Schwung can keep an older copy of a module in memory.

Releases are built by GitHub Actions on every v* tag. The tarball in the release is what the Schwung module catalogue installs.

PlayFirst sound in a minute

  1. Hold Track 1-4 for half a second to open a slot, and choose VDP33 as the sound generator.
  2. On that Move track set MIDI Out to a channel from 1 to 4, check the slot's Receive Ch matches, and load an empty Drum Rack so the native sound stays quiet.
  3. Play the pads. The first preset, Strings, needs no microphone.
  4. For the vocoder pick the Vocoder preset, plug in headphones or a line-in cable, and speak.
The vocoder listens to the Move's audio input. With the built-in speaker on and nothing plugged in, Schwung's feedback guard bypasses the slot and a B appears. Plug in headphones or a cable and it comes back by itself. The Move has one input, so there is no separate external synth input: Ext In is the same signal as the vocoder modulator and shares Mic Level.

The screen has nine pages of up to eight knobs: Main, Balance, Keyboard, Organ, Strings, Envelope, Ensemble, Vibrato and Pitch. Turn a knob and its value is shown in real units (milliseconds, cents, semitones).

The panelWhere everything lives

Balance and output

Strings, Human Voice, Organ and Direct Mic are the four faders. Levels follow a square law, so the lower half of a fader is a quiet region. Ext In to Ensemble sends the audio input through the ensemble untouched. Master Tone tilts the whole mix darker or brighter around 1.2 kHz, up to 6 dB either way. Volume is the master.

Vocoder

Vocoder Keys chooses Off, Lower, Upper or All; Lower and Upper are split at Split Point. Mic Level is input gain from -12 to +24 dB, Vocoder Tone a brightness control for the carrier, Sibilance adds a noise path so consonants stay clear, and Ensemble sends the vocoder through the chorus.

Human voice

Four switches pick the choirs: Male 8' Lower, Male 4' Lower, Male 8' Upper and Female 4' Upper. Attack sets how fast they swell and Ensemble sends them through the chorus.

Strings RS-09

Register is VP-330 (the original 16', 8' and 4' mix), 8' alone, 4' alone, or 8' + 4'. Tone darkens or opens the strings, Attack is their swell, and Attack on legato decides whether every new key swells again (Each key) or only the first key of a phrase does (First key). The strings always run through the ensemble.

Organ RS-09

Four faders mix the feet 8', 4', 2' and 1' of square waves. Tone is I Soft or II Bright, Ensemble sends the organ through the chorus, and Vibrato on organ lets the vibrato reach it. Organ Keys picks Off, Lower, Upper or All, so strings can sit under the split and an organ above it.

Envelope RS-09

One Release fader serves all sections. Release mode has three settings:

  • Free: each key owns one envelope, and a key you replay picks its own tail up again. This is how VDP33 behaved before 0.3.
  • I Wait: when no key is held, a new note waits until the old tail has faded below about -35 dB.
  • II Hold: a replayed key gets a new voice while the old tail finishes on its own.

The editor draws the envelope of two notes for the mode you have chosen.

Ensemble RS-09

Ensemble Mode is Standard (the original), I Dynamic (deeper, faster and wetter) or II Natural (shallower and calmer). Depth and Speed are fine trims on top.

Vibrato

Rate from 2 to 9 Hz, Delay up to two seconds before it fades in, Depth up to ±60 cents. Vibrato always reaches Human Voice and Vocoder. Vibrato on strings and Vibrato on organ let it reach those sections too.

Pitch shift and tuning VP-330

Pitch Shift Mode is Off/Pedal, Auto or Manual. In Auto, each key press drops the pitch by Pitch Set and it glides back up over Time. Manual holds a static shift set by Pitch Down. Off/Pedal does nothing until CC 66 is held, then it acts like Auto. The shift now reaches all three sections. Tune is ±50 cents, and Reference A switches 440 to 442 Hz. Bend Range and Octave complete the section.

Keyboard and pedals

Split Point divides Lower from Upper. Velocity is Fixed or Dynamic. Pedal (CC 64) has four settings:

  • Off: the pedal does nothing.
  • Gate: the vocoder sounds only while the pedal is down (the old VDP33 behaviour).
  • Hold: a true Vocoder Hold. Pressing it freezes the ten band levels, so the vocal colour keeps sounding without any input.
  • Release: the RS-09 Release pedal. With the pedal up, release is zero; with it down, the Release fader applies.

Updates in 0.3What came from the RS-09 and the VP-330 manual

From the Roland RS-09

  • An Organ section with 8', 4', 2' and 1' faders, tone I and II, and its own ensemble switch.
  • Strings registers 8' and 4', separate from the VP-330 mix.
  • Ensemble modes I and II, release modes I and II, the release pedal and the legato attack choice.
  • Ext In through the ensemble, a master Tone, a 442 Hz reference, and vibrato switches for organ and strings.
  • Nine presets, numbers 15 to 23, named RS .... The first fifteen are unchanged.

Corrected from the VP-330 owner's manual

  • Pitch shift reaches strings, voice and organ, not only voice and vocoder.
  • Tune is ±50 cents. It had been ±1 semitone.
  • Vocoder Hold now freezes the band levels. The old Gate behaviour stays as Gate.

Old saved states load as before: parameters that did not exist are filled with neutral defaults, and Release mode defaults to Free, so a patch made in 0.2 sounds like it did.

Web editorThe same panel in a browser

With VDP33 in a slot, open Schwung Manager at http://move.local:7700 and choose Remote UI. VDP33's own editor appears there: fader and knobs for every parameter, the preset list, and a picture of the envelope. Everything follows the Move as you turn its knobs, and the Move follows the browser.

The same page runs as a demo without a Move: open the demo editor. A rough on-screen keyboard plays a simplified browser version of the Organ and Strings so you can see what the controls do; Human Voice and the Vocoder are silent there, and the real sound is on the Move.

Mouse: drag a knob or fader up and down, use the wheel, double-click to reset, hold Shift for fine steps. Keyboard: arrows, Page Up and Page Down, Home and End.

HonestyWhat the manuals say, and what is a guess

Confirmed manuals

  • The section layout, four Human Voice switches, one shared Release.
  • Separate Attack for voice and strings; Tone on strings and vocoder.
  • Organ feet, strings registers, ensemble and release modes, release pedal and legato attack exist on the RS-09.
  • Pitch shift reaches all sections; Tune is ±50 cents; Vocoder Hold exists.

Estimates not measured

  • Centre frequencies and Q of the ten bands; choir formants; register mixes.
  • Ensemble delay times, rates and the two mode scalings.
  • Organ tone filters and foot weights.
  • The meaning of Release Mode I and II. The RS-09 manual words it ambiguously. I implemented I as Wait and II as Hold, and kept Free as the default.

Not modelled: the Gate Out and Organ Raw Out jacks, the L/M/H output switch, a second input, SysEx, analogue drift and BBD companding noise. The constants most worth tuning against a real unit are k_band_hz and BAND_Q, the register mix in vdp_render, the formant tables and the ensemble constants; they are isolated and commented in src/dsp/vdp33.c.

The native test, bash scripts/test.sh, renders every preset to WAV and checks pitch, levels, vocoder tracking, Hold, zones, organ feet and tone, strings registers, release modes, legato attack, pedals, ensemble modes, master tone, old-state compatibility and CPU load.
